## 2. Recommendation-grading audit — A/B/C/E letters

- Every "Recommendations" bullet in the source carries a parenthetical letter, e.g. `(A)`, `(B)`, `(C)`, `(E)` (examples: MD:594, 598, 600, 602, 608, 612, 614, 616, 636–648, 664–686, 736–744, 786–794).
- **Finding — no in-text definition of the grading scale.** I searched both artifacts (`grep -i` on the parsed Markdown and `pdftotext -layout` full-text search of the PDF) for any legend, footnote, or table defining what A/B/C/E mean (evidence tier vs. strength of recommendation vs. expert opinion). None was found in either file. No "Table 3"-style grading-definition table is present in the parsed content.
- The letters are consistent with (but not explicitly cited as) the **ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes** evidence-grading convention (A = clear RCT evidence, B = supportive cohort-study evidence, C = supportive evidence from poorly controlled/uncontrolled studies, E = expert consensus/clinical experience), and the panel does cite an ADA Standards of Care chapter elsewhere (ref [40], MD:952). This is a **plausible but unverified inference**, not a confirmed fact — flag it as such in any downstream deliverable ("推測依循 ADA 慣例，原文未明示定義" / "presumed to follow the ADA convention; the source article does not define the scale").
- **Consequence for downstream memos:** Per BRIEF §Source policy item 3, `t1d-adult` and `t1d-safety` memos must not present a bracketed `(A)`/`(B)`/`(C)`/`(E)` letter as equivalent to a GRADE `high/moderate/low/very low` certainty rating. The two are different systems; the source paper's A/B/C/E marks the panel's *strength/basis of recommendation*, not an independently reproducible quality-of-evidence assessment. Many `(E)`-graded recommendations in the source concern **operational/behavioral guidance** (e.g., follow-up cadence, patient education content) rather than efficacy claims, and several **(A)-graded** items (e.g., MD:608, 612 — "use of CGM," "reassess insulin regimens at every visit") reflect well-established standard-of-care practice orthogonal to the GLP-1/GIP question itself, not GLP-1/GIP-specific RCT evidence. Grade letters must therefore be read next to the specific claim they attach to, not treated as a document-wide quality score.

## 4. Observational-study bias assessment

The source itself flags, in prose (MD:450, 476), several biases relevant to its own real-world evidence base — this audit confirms and extends them:

1. **Selection bias in prescribing.** MD:476 explicitly notes: *"Selection biases for GLP-1 RA prescribing could also be a factor for the absence of acute diabetes events in real-world studies."* Clinicians likely selected lower-risk, more motivated, or better-resourced patients for off-label GLP-1/GIP use — this alone could explain part of the favorable safety signal (low DKA/severe hypoglycemia rates) without any causal effect of the drug.
2. **Underreporting / surveillance bias.** MD:476: real-world studies "may underreport instances of severe hypoglycemia or ketosis, as participants are not explicitly monitored for these events," unlike prospective RCT protocols (ADJUNCT ONE/TWO) that had structured AE ascertainment.
3. **Healthy-adherer / immortal-time bias risk** in the TriNetX (ref 56) and target-trial-emulation (ref 57) cohort analyses is plausible given retrospective drug-exposure cohort designs, though the source does not report the specific bias-mitigation methods used (e.g., new-user design, landmark analysis, negative control outcomes) in the body text available here — cannot confirm or rule out from local sources; the Nat Med methods section (ref 57) was not independently retrieved in this run (no internet access exercised; per BRIEF §Source policy, no unauthorized download was attempted).
4. **Heterogeneous comparator/dose/agent pooling.** Several Table 1 rows pool participants on different agents (e.g., Gonzalez: "Semaglutide Dulaglutide Liraglutide," Almohareb: "Semaglutide Liraglutide," MD:279–294, 415–430) — effect estimates for "GLP-1 RA" as a class conflate agents with materially different potency/GI tolerability profiles (tirzepatide vs. semaglutide vs. older daily agents), which downstream memos should not present as a single homogeneous effect size.
5. **Short/variable follow-up, small N.** Most Table 1 arms are 24–141 participants over 12–24 months; only 3 of 8 extend beyond 12 months (16, 18, 21 months — MD:442). This limits inference about long-term durability, late-onset AEs (e.g., DR progression, sarcopenia), or rare safety events.
6. **No risk-of-bias tool cited.** The "Search Strategy and Selection Criteria" section (MD:840–846) describes the literature search (MEDLINE/PubMed/Cochrane, to Feb 9, 2026, defined Boolean terms) and defines RCT/real-world/observational/retrospective study categories, but does not report use of a formal risk-of-bias instrument (e.g., Cochrane RoB2 for RCTs, Newcastle-Ottawa Scale for cohort studies) or a PRISMA-style flow diagram. This is consistent with a **narrative expert consensus review**, not a systematic review — downstream deliverables should describe it as such and not imply systematic-review-level rigor.

## 6. Verification of C-01 and C-02 against the primary source

### C-01 — `200 mg/dL` vs. `13.9 mmol/L` unit-conversion mismatch — **CONFIRMED, internal to the source document**

- Body text, page ≈8 (`MD:580`): *"...monitor ketone levels when glucose levels are **≥200 mg/dL (≥11.1 mmol/L)** for 2 h or more."* (cites ref [73], Umpierrez et al., *Diabetologia* 2024;67(8):1455–1479, a hyperglycemic-crises consensus report). **200 mg/dL ÷ 18.0182 = 11.10 mmol/L — this conversion is arithmetically correct.**
- Recommendations bullet, page ≈9 (`MD:602`): *"Ketone levels should be monitored when glucose levels are **≥200 mg/dL (≥13.9 mmol/L)** for 2 h or more. (B) Ketone monitoring is also required during illness... even if glucose values are **<250 mg/dL** (euglycemic ketosis). (B)"* **13.9 mmol/L × 18.0182 ≈ 250.5 mg/dL — i.e., 13.9 mmol/L is the correct conversion for 250 mg/dL, not 200 mg/dL**, and 250 mg/dL is in fact the very next number used in the same bullet (for the euglycemic-ketosis threshold).
- **Conclusion:** this is a genuine internal inconsistency in the published consensus report, most plausibly an editing artifact where the 250 mg/dL→13.9 mmol/L conversion was carried over into the restatement of the 200 mg/dL threshold. The correct, internally-consistent value for the "check ketones when glucose ≥200 mg/dL" rule is **11.1 mmol/L**, matching the body text and the cited primary source's usual convention. **Recommendation for downstream deliverables:** state the rule as "≥200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L)" and add an explicit footnote flagging that the source's Recommendations-list parenthetical (13.9 mmol/L) is an apparent transcription error, do not silently "correct" it without disclosure, and do not present both numbers as if they were two different valid thresholds.

### C-02 — Graphical-abstract "no change for upper endoscopy" vs. main-text individualized/OCULUS-based guidance — **CONFIRMED, internal to the source document**

- Graphical abstract conclusion, page ≈3–4 (`MD:149`): *"For upper endoscopy, no change in GLP/GIP1-RA needed."* — stated as a categorical, universal rule with no caveats.
- Main text, Perioperative Management, page ≈13 (`MD:728`): describes the **OCULUS RCT** (ref 149, Ahmad/Garg/Jacobs et al., *JAMA Intern Med* 2026;186(5):578–584) — of 60 adults on stable-dose GLP-1/GIP scheduled for endoscopy, those who **continued** therapy (n=28) had inadequate visualization due to retained gastric content in **25%** of cases, versus **3.1%** in those who **held** one dose beforehand (n=32); difference significant at P=0.0029. The same paragraph also cites two other primary reports of higher gastric residual volumes in patients continuing GLP-1/GIP for endoscopy (refs 147, 148).
- Main text, Recommendations, page ≈13 (`MD:738`): *"Ahead of planned procedure or surgery, multiple professional societies recommend using an **individualized approach**, with shared decision making among individuals living with T1D, proceduralists, endocrinologists, and anesthesiologists, to stopping versus continuing GLP-1 and GLP-1/GIP RA. (E)"* — no categorical "no change" rule appears anywhere in the formal Recommendations bullets; the closest the body text comes to universal guidance is recommending a **clear liquid diet 24 h before the procedure regardless of hold/continue status** (MD:740).
- **Conclusion:** the graphical abstract's blanket statement directly contradicts (a) the RCT evidence the panel itself cites (OCULUS: continuing therapy meant ~8× higher rate of inadequate visualization) and (b) the panel's own formal, individualized recommendation. This is an internal inconsistency, most likely arising because the graphical abstract was drafted/illustrated separately (credited to a named illustrator, "Robert Brines," MD:155) and not reconciled against the final body text and OCULUS citation. **Recommendation for downstream deliverables:** do not reproduce the graphical abstract's "no change for upper endoscopy" line as a stand-alone clinical instruction. State instead that the panel recommends an individualized, shared decision-making approach for endoscopy as for other procedures, and cite the OCULUS finding (continuing therapy associated with a significantly higher rate of inadequate gastric visualization) as the primary evidence informing that individualization — this is the safer, source-consistent wording.
